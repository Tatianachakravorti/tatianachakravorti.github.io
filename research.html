Research

I study how people use AI in scholarly work, how they assess research, and how AI can be used responsibly.

01

Human-centered AI

I study how people interact with AI systems, when they choose to rely on or delegate tasks to AI, and how collaboration affects professional judgment.

Hybrid prediction markets & research workflows

As a graduate research assistant at Penn State, I worked on the DARPA Systematizing Confidence in Open Research and Evidence (SCORE) project from 2021 to 2022. I developed hybrid prediction markets where people traded alongside AI-enabled agents to forecast whether published research would replicate. I also designed collaboration features for an AI-assisted replicability prediction tool, using researcher needs to guide the design, and tested its usability.

Researchers’ perspectives on AI

In the AI for Synthesizing Social Science Knowledge project at Penn State (2024–2025), I used interviews and surveys to understand where social scientists saw opportunities for AI and what ethical concerns they had about using it in their research.

02

AI Ethics

My research examines transparency, accountability, trust, and responsible AI use across scholarly and educational settings.

Responsible AI for peer review

At Penn State (2025–2026), I interviewed AI journal editors and reviewers about how they use, evaluate, or avoid large language models in peer review. I followed the interviews with quantitative analysis to examine the implications for judgment, effort, and research ethics.

Current projects · University of Alabama

NSF EAGER

Securing AI Research: A Lifecycle-Based Typology of Threats

I am developing a typology of security threats across the AI research lifecycle. The study uses Critical Incident Technique interviews with principal investigators, cybersecurity personnel, compliance and export control officers, research administrators, and federal partners, alongside a national stakeholder survey.

I combine qualitative analysis with NLP methods to identify threat patterns and develop scenarios grounded in reported incidents. These scenarios will be validated with stakeholders from different sectors in a participatory workshop.

NSF funded

Mapping AI Ethics Education

I contribute to a multi-institution study of AI ethics education across U.S. institutions. Using a sequential explanatory mixed-methods design and a national survey, we map curriculum priorities, assessment strategies, and teaching approaches.

We examine how faculty perceptions and institutional needs and capacities shape AI ethics education. Our partner networks include research universities, community colleges, and minority serving institutions.

NSF funded

AI Ethics Bowl: Evaluating Students’ Ethical Reasoning about AI

This project uses the AI Ethics Bowl to teach AI ethics in undergraduate STEM courses through real-world case studies and team debate. In a pre/post evaluation, students became more familiar with AI and ethics. Their responses also moved from naming AI harms to reasoning through ethical principles such as autonomy, justice, and responsibility.

I am developing a semi-structured interview protocol to examine how team deliberation and public argumentation shape students’ ethical judgment and their views on delegating decisions to AI.

03

Science of Science

I investigate reproducibility, replicability, Open Science, and the ways researchers assess scientific credibility.

Research credibility & Open Science

At Penn State, I interviewed and observed researchers to understand their experiences with the reproducibility crisis and Open Science. I also designed and statistically analyzed a survey of researchers from different geographic regions.

My publications include a study of 452 professors at universities in the USA and India and a study of Open Science practices among early-career HCI researchers in the US. I have also worked on the opportunities and challenges of incorporating replicability measures into scholarly search.

Earlier engineering research

As a research assistant at Siksha ’O’ Anusandhan University (2015–2018), I developed signal processing and machine learning methods to detect and classify microgrid disturbances. In 2017, I worked as a Project Assistant Intern on the LINDA project at the University of Applied Sciences Augsburg. There, I analyzed field data from local island power supply systems during large-scale blackout scenarios.
